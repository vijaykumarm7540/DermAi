# Skin Disease Knowledge Base for DermAI

## Melanoma
- **Description**: A serious type of skin cancer that develops from the pigment-producing cells (melanocytes). It is more dangerous than other skin cancers because it has a higher tendency to spread to other parts of the body if not treated early.
- **Clinical Signs**: Asymmetry, irregular borders, multiple colors, diameter > 6mm, and evolving size/shape (ABCDE criteria).
- **Severity**: High (Severe).
- **Recommendation**: Immediate surgical excision is the primary treatment. Requires urgent referral to a dermatologist and potentially an oncologist for staging (e.g., lymph node biopsy).
- **Precautions**: Avoid UV exposure, use broad-spectrum sunscreen (SPF 50+), and perform regular total body skin exams.

## Melanocytic Nevus (Mole)
- **Description**: A common non-cancerous skin growth (mole). Most moles appear in childhood and early adulthood. They are generally harmless.
- **Clinical Signs**: Usually round or oval, uniform in color (brown, tan, or black), and have smooth borders.
- **Severity**: Low (Mild).
- **Recommendation**: No active treatment required unless it changes or for cosmetic reasons.
- **Precautions**: Monitor for any changes in ABCDE criteria. If a mole starts itching, bleeding, or growing rapidly, consult a doctor.

## Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)
- **Description**: The most common form of skin cancer. It grows slowly and rarely spreads (metastasizes), but can be locally destructive if left untreated.
- **Clinical Signs**: Shiny, pearly bumps, often with visible blood vessels (telangiectasia). May look like a sore that bleeds and doesn't heal.
- **Severity**: Moderate to High.
- **Recommendation**: Treatment includes Mohs surgery, standard excision, or topical therapies. Usually has a very high cure rate when caught early.
- **Precautions**: Regular follow-ups as patients with one BCC are at risk for developing others.

## Actinic Keratosis
- **Description**: A precancerous skin growth caused by damage from UV rays. If left untreated, it can develop into squamous cell carcinoma.
- **Clinical Signs**: Rough, scaly patches on sun-exposed areas like the face, scalp, or hands.
- **Severity**: Moderate.
- **Recommendation**: Cryotherapy (freezing), topical creams (e.g., Efudex), or photodynamic therapy.
- **Precautions**: Consistent sun protection is critical to prevent new lesions.

## Benign Keratosis-like Lesion (Seborrheic Keratosis)
- **Description**: Non-cancerous skin growths that are common in older adults. They are not contagious and do not become cancerous.
- **Clinical Signs**: Waxy, "stuck-on" appearance, ranging in color from light tan to black. Often have a crumbly surface.
- **Severity**: Low (Mild).
- **Recommendation**: No treatment needed. Can be removed by a dermatologist if they become irritated by clothing.
- **Precautions**: None, though they can sometimes mimic the appearance of melanoma to the untrained eye.

## Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)
- **Description**: The second most common form of skin cancer. It arises from the squamous cells in the epidermis.
- **Clinical Signs**: Scaly red patches, open sores, or elevated growths with a central depression. Often crusty or bleed.
- **Severity**: High (Moderate to Severe).
- **Recommendation**: Surgical excision or Mohs surgery. High risk of recurrence or spread if large or deep.
- **Precautions**: Strict sun avoidance and regular dermatological check-ups.
